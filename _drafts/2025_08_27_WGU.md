## In the beginning

I've had a fairly successful career up until 2020 where my lack of production experience in Kubernetes crushed most of my opportunities. Even gaining my Certified Kubernetes Administrator certification wasn't helpful without real workload experience.

Something that has been pointed out, that I don't want to believe, is that my lack of a higher degree is getting me filtered out of the deep pool of applicants.

So, I've decided, I wanted to reboot my career now that I don't need to do DevOps any longer, it's time to get started.  I'm willing to get back into student loan debt (something I escaped only a few years ago) to shake things up.

After looking at my options, WGU stands out for it's self-paced coursework. It's more expensive than other options, but it has a good reputation and I might be able to finish sooner that trying to fit in-person classes into a work schedule.

Pursuing the online Bachelors offerings, between Computer Science and Data Analytics, I choose that later to have a little less math to accomplish and because D.A. is applicable across a lot of industries with the massive amounts of data we are processing these days.

## Access Denied

## Breezing through the beginning.

The first three (of seven) modules are all review of basic number terminology. operations order and methods, sets and groups. So by the time I met with my instructor, five days after I started, I was already beginning module 5. She told me to not hesitate to reach out if the material becomes more challenging, but to keep on working my way through the course at my own pace.

She did confirm my calculator (which supports Python, something I missed when I bought it) is suitable for the proctored exams, and showed me some functions on the device that will be useful in the D.A. program.

Early the next week, I met with my Student Specialist for the Introductory Term (it will be someone else once I'm in the full program) at which point I had already started module 7. She wished me luck in finishing and offered her support.

I am aware that the rapid pace I'm accomplishing this course is in large part thanks to the Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning course I tried to take last year. While I barely passed that class due to it's sudden use of statistics, the class exposed me to most of these terms and concepts.

It took just over two weeks to finish the main content. Some of my mistakes in the practice exam are from misunderstanding/misreading the question (slow down!), however, I still passed with > 90%.  I plan on taking the exam well-rested and when the household is at school.

## updates

I lost the fledgling habit of blogging, so lets try to catch up...

As I said in my last update, I breezed through most of The Data to Analytics course because of what I learned previously. So I finished the course in two weeks.

Next up, a natural science lab where I had to perform a scientific experiment that had a manipulatable input and a falsifyable prediction and can't involve an animal with a spine.  I choose that adding a lid to a heating vessel will decrease the heating time to a boil.

My first round of experiments, I tried to use an 'instant' read thermometer every 15 seconds to record the temperature.  This was challenging: trying to get the probe to the bottom of the water without touching the bottom, waiting a few seconds for the temperature to adjust (even though it was also rising), don't get burned from steam. To say that this was 'rough' measurements is being kind.

Also, I obviously couldn't take temperatures with the lid off, so I couldn't record the temperature progression during experiments with full lid coverage.

I decided to scrap the results and start over. I bought a wireless cooking thermometer, placed it into a sealed sous vide pouch with the air vacuumed out.

Then I reran the experiements and was able to get much more consistent results,

Of note, even though the water was boiling, vessels without a lid never reached more than 209.2°F and of the experiments with a lid, one actually reached 212.2.

This was only supposed to take a week, but because of issues with waiting to receive things from amazon, it took 2.5 weeks, but that's still faster than the month allocated to a class.

### Change

It should be plainly clear that Analytics can be used to suggest changes to an organization.  As such, there is a clear need for the ability to discuss and report on the change process that hopefully occurs to act on the analysis.

This class requires me to create a plan to assist a theoritical company make a big change.

My problem, the scenario is presented entirely in only 6 sentances.  I have to make believe that there is a company, make up the data and stakeholders, and present that in a tightly defined format, using only the course materials to complete the task.  The project must also pass a uniqueness metric from an AI scoring tool that shows a similarity score.

This class finally gets the walls of text I heard about.  I didn't expect things to stay a breeze.

This project takes too much time but passes on the first submission.

### Scripting

### Critical thinking

I still have over a month left in this term, so I start another class, this time on Critical Thinking.

This class goes into depth on arguments, premises and conclusions. Mix in a long list of fallacies and end with a project that analyzes a bad argument.

I got MOST of the project correct, but my project was returned for further enhancment.

After mulling on the problem for about 15 minutes, I scheduled my first meeting with the instructor.  He was able to help me understand my mistake and my resubmitted project was accepted.

### Version control
