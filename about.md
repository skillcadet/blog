I like figuring out how systems fit together, especially when the problem crosses the boundaries between software, infrastructure, and the people using it. My work has included software development, operations, hosting, startups, and decentralized infrastructure.

## How I got here

My first technology job was rewriting point-of-sale and back-office software for a fuel-station company while I was in college. It got me hooked on solving problems with code.

I later moved into operations because I wanted to understand what happened after software was written: how it was deployed, maintained, and kept running. In 1995, I co-launched a web-hosting business, designed its technical stack, and helped customers establish an internet presence.

From there I worked across startups, decentralized projects, infrastructure automation, and large clusters. That experience changed how I approach application development. A design isn't finished when the code works on my machine; someone needs to deploy it, operate it, troubleshoot it, and eventually hand it over.

## How I work now

I've been spending more time building applications again, often with AI-assisted development tools. They help me move quickly, particularly when I'm working in languages or frameworks I haven't used in years. But the generated code still needs scrutiny. I care about understanding its assumptions, testing the edge cases, and fixing the operational details that a quick prototype tends to miss.

I enjoy the work that connects technical choices to an actual person's problem: listening to what isn't working, finding the constraints, and putting together something useful and maintainable. I also like explaining the reasoning, including the mistakes.

The [homepage]({{ '/' | relative_url }}) links to current projects and writing. This page is the background; the articles are where I get into the implementation.
